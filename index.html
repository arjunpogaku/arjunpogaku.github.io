Question
Which stations in Fukushima had the worst air last winter?
Grounded by
  • Fukushima means the canonical value prefecture = 'Fukushima'
  • last winter means December to February
  • worst air means highest mean PM2.5
Cypher
MATCH (s:Station)-[:RECORDED]->(o:Observation)
WHERE s.prefecture = 'Fukushima'
  AND o.month IN [12, 1, 2]
RETURN s.name, avg(o.pm25) AS mean
ORDER BY mean DESC LIMIT 5
Returns
Five stations ranked by winter mean, each traceable to the hourly observations behind it.

What I work on

Knowledge graphs
Turning raw spatiotemporal readings into explicit nodes and relationships, so an answer can be traced back to the data that produced it.
Pattern mining
Finding meaningful, uncertain, and time-dependent patterns in large real-world datasets, and the systems work that makes searching them at scale practical.
Grounded language models
Constraining LLMs with schemas and domain rules so they generate queries and workflows that actually run, instead of plausible guesses.

Selected work

JPM2KG and AirCypher-150

BDA 2026, accepted and presented

A knowledge graph of more than 71 million air-quality observations across 1,116 monitoring stations in Japan, and an execution-validated benchmark of 150 questions for querying it in plain language. Prompting with the schema alone produced queries that ran but were almost never correct; adding structured domain knowledge is what closed the gap.

A symbolic knowledge graph for traffic congestion

IEA/AIE 2026, Springer Nature

Deterministic rules turn high-frequency congestion readings into a graph of spatial, temporal, and congestion-level relationships, and a domain-constrained LLM generates Cypher directly from a natural-language question — an interpretable alternative to purely predictive traffic models.

About

I am a researcher working at the intersection of pattern mining, knowledge graphs, and large language models. I was born in 1997 in Madanapalle, Andhra Pradesh, India.

I am pursuing my Ph.D. in the Graduate Department of Computer and Information Systems at the University of Aizu, Japan, under the supervision of Dr. Uday Kiran RAGE. My research centers on grounding LLMs in structured, verifiable knowledge. Alongside my own projects, I contribute to research led by colleagues on pattern mining at scale.

Before this, I worked on wireless communications and reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) at the WICOM Lab, Asia University, and served as an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering at the Madanapalle Institute of Technology and Science (MITS), India.
