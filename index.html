Biography

Arjun Chakravarthi Pogaku, born in 1997 in Madanapalle, Andhra Pradesh, India, is a researcher working at the intersection of Pattern Mining, Knowledge Graphs, and Large Language Models.

He is currently pursuing his Ph.D. in the Graduate Department of Computer and Information Systems at the University of Aizu, Japan, under the supervision of Dr. Uday Kiran RAGE.

His current research centers on grounding LLMs in structured, verifiable knowledge for data mining. With PAMI-GPT, he built a conversational framework that turns natural-language descriptions of pattern-mining workflows into reliable, executable analyses — work since formalized into a knowledge-grounded LLM framework for pattern mining. In parallel, he developed a symbolic knowledge graph framework for traffic congestion analysis, pairing deterministic graph construction with a domain-constrained LLM that generates Cypher queries directly from natural-language questions — an interpretable alternative to purely predictive traffic models. His broader pattern-mining work spans GPU-accelerated fuzzy frequent pattern mining and open datasets for real-world discovery, including a published dataset on PM2.5 pollution trends in Japan. That record now underpins JPM2KG, a knowledge graph of more than 71 million air-quality observations, and AirCypher-150, a benchmark for natural-language querying over it, accepted and presented at BDA 2026.

Before this, Arjun contributed to research in Wireless Communications and Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS) as part of the WICOM Lab at Asia University, and served as an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electronics and Communication Engineering at the Madanapalle Institute of Technology and Science (MITS), India.
